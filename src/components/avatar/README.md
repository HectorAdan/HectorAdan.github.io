# Holographic avatar study

Open `?avatar=prototype` on the existing site. This separate, lazy-loaded preview does not replace the Neural Core or change portfolio navigation.

The temporary bust is original procedural geometry. It represents a generic head and shoulders, not Hector's likeness. There are no photographs, textures, talking animation, external model services, or new dependencies.

## Model boundary

`avatarSurface.ts` prepares either the procedural bust or a GLB as one normalized `BufferGeometry`. It bakes visible mesh transforms and the current skin/morph pose, combines surfaces, centers them, and normalizes their height to 3.8 scene units. `sampleAvatar.ts` samples that geometry with Three.js `MeshSurfaceSampler`. `HolographicAvatar.tsx` renders the same buffers for either source.

To use the future personalized model:

1. Export an **uncompressed, self-contained** head-and-shoulders GLB with Y up and the face looking toward +Z. Apply appropriate neutral pose, remove cameras/lights and hidden accessories, and keep geometry reasonably light (preferably below 50,000 triangles). Avoid a full body mesh; framing is designed for a bust.
2. Place the file at `public/hector-avatar.glb`.
3. Open `?avatar=prototype&model=hector`. The loader uses Vite's `BASE_URL`, including a GitHub Pages subdirectory if configured. If loading fails, the preview identifies the fallback and displays the temporary bust.

The source model's materials and textures are discarded after preparation; holographic shading is generated by the renderer. Draco/Meshopt compression requires extra decoder setup and is intentionally not enabled. No animation clips are played. Integrating the approved avatar into the main portfolio is a separate step.

## Rendering and accessibility

- 11,000 surface particles on desktop; 4,500 on compact/coarse-pointer devices. Three avatar draw calls (points, sparse pathways, translucent shell), plus two projector meshes. No bloom or post-processing passes.
- Spatial hashing builds local connections among a subset of particles; each has at most two connections, capped at 1,000 desktop / 430 compact edges. Energy travels along those segments using shader uniforms. The facial geometry does not animate.
- DPR is capped and can downshift once after warm-up on slow devices. Animation pauses in hidden tabs. OS reduced motion and the pause button use demand rendering; manual camera exploration still works.
- Front, three-quarter and profile buttons provide keyboard-accessible camera views. Dragging uses Three.js OrbitControls. WebGL errors and context loss show a route back to the working portfolio.
- Loader-owned surfaces, sampled buffers, source materials and textures, and controls are disposed when no longer needed.

The study is intentionally isolated for review. Further anatomical refinement and a real personalized model will improve identity and expression without changing the particle renderer.

Open `?avatar=prototype&model=mediapipe`, choose **Load facial mask**, and select the private `avatar-references/mediapipe-poc/output/hector-mediapipe-poc.glb` file. The browser reads this self-contained GLB through a temporary object URL; it is never uploaded or copied to `public/`. This mode loads only the chosen facial mask and never creates a fallback bust. The default prototype and `model=hector` mode are preserved.

## Portfolio introduction

The home page's **Meet Hector** action uses `canvas/NeuralIdentity.tsx` to hand off the original Neural Core to the existing holographic renderer. The public model at `models/hector-mediapipe-poc.glb` matches the private proof-of-concept GLB; no photograph or skin texture is embedded. It loads through Vite's `BASE_URL`. To upgrade later, change `IDENTITY_MODEL_PATH` to another self-contained neutral GLB with Y up and +Z facing forward. `avatarSurface.ts` still provides the common normalization boundary.

`utils/identityIntroduction.ts` describes the activation, Core fade, facial reveal and particle assembly timing. Core activation starts immediately; the camera approaches over about 1.1 seconds. From .8 to 4.6 seconds, a compact separate cloud converges onto the sampled facial surface while the Core recedes. Eyes, nose, brows and mouth settle ahead of the outer contours. This is a staged transition, not a deformation of the Core's original points. Closing restores the original presentation; replay starts from the intact Core and restarts both particle and text reveals. The greeting is visible immediately, independently of model loading. The face stays mostly front-facing with gentle idle tilt and damped cursor following (about 13 degrees horizontally and 8 degrees vertically). Mouse input is measured relative to the projected face center through a passive window listener, so it works over the DOM introduction and its controls, including on narrow desktop windows. Tracking fades in as assembly settles and returns toward neutral when the pointer leaves, the window loses focus, or the page is hidden. Touch input is ignored; reduced motion disables all tracking. Listeners are removed when the introduction closes. There is no orbit control or new facial reconstruction. The home introduction now reuses the isolated talking preview’s corrected morphs and audio-driven speech animation.

The introduction opts into `emergingIdentity` on the shared renderer. The facial mask uses even surface sampling so the mouth and jaw retain enough particles; the isolated bust keeps its original sampling weights. The spatial shader field preserves a continuous central face and limits fragmentation to the perimeter after assembly, with very slow drift confined to sparse fringe particles. Normalized feature bands control reveal timing and tint only; they never reshape the source geometry. A future model with significantly different framing may need these bands tuned. The introductory surface layer uses a dark cyan tint at 6% of the isolated study's already translucent shell opacity (roughly .5–2.3% actual alpha before the edge fade), providing faint facial depth between particles. Thin cyan/blue pathways now carry the main facial structure, reusing the environment’s electrical pulse shader. Evenly sampled nodes have at most three local links with aligned surface normals; source boundary segments additionally trace the eye and mouth openings and outer contour. Connected nodes glow softly; unconnected samples remain faint depth dust. Restrained violet accents preserve the existing palette. A sparse atmospheric point layer softens the incomplete perimeter without creating skull, hair, neck or shoulders. The original isolated preview keeps its default particle shading, shell, projector and camera controls.

Performance: 3,800 face particles / at most 2,200 pathways on desktop; 2,200 / at most 1,200 on compact devices, including a bounded contour budget. The isolated study retains its original 11,000 / 4,500 particles and 1,000 / 430 connection caps. Atmosphere adds 180 / 72 points. Four identity draw calls and no post-processing; Core and face overlap briefly during the handoff. Geometry is sampled once per source/quality change; animation uses refs and uniforms, with no per-frame React state updates. Hidden tabs stop rendering. Reduced motion skips camera travel, particle assembly and idle motion, and reveals the text immediately. The compact neural face is used on mobile with face-above-text framing and a scrollable text area on short screens.

If the GLB fails to load, the Core remains available and the text introduction still works. If WebGL is unavailable, the existing lightweight environment carries the introduction and all portfolio navigation. The model remains a coarse single-photo facial mask with empty eyes/mouth and no side anatomy. Holographic presentation does not correct its depth or guarantee a strong likeness.

Checks: `npm run build`, `node scripts/check-avatar.mjs`, and `node scripts/check-identity.mjs`. Browser checks cover Meet Hector, replay, closing/Escape, navigation after introduction, production asset loading, mobile framing, reduced motion and missing-model/WebGL fallback.

Run `node scripts/check-avatar.mjs` to validate geometry bounds, normalized normals, deterministic sampling, local connection lengths and degree caps, and preparation of transformed mixed meshes. `npm run build` validates TypeScript and the static production build.

## Isolated talking preview

Open `?avatar=talking`, choose the original 28-second introduction MP4 from the ignored `avatar-references/` directory, and press **Play introduction**. The browser decodes its audio locally; an audio element plays the recording through a temporary object URL. No video frames are displayed or uploaded. The recording is deliberately not bundled into `public/` or committed, so a reload requires choosing it again. The same file-selection workflow works on GitHub Pages without a backend. WAV, MP3 and M4A files can also be used when a browser cannot decode audio from a video container.

Play, pause, replay and the keyboard-accessible playback slider share `audio.currentTime`. The particle toggle switches between the deforming neural hologram and a translucent surface comparison. Caption text comes from Hector's supplied transcript; sentence times were estimated from measured silence intervals in the reference recording, rather than speech recognition or word alignment. A different recording can use locally loaded WebVTT captions. No synthetic voice or paid API is used.

`talkingSurface.ts` / `localizedMorphs.ts` validate the three nonzero named targets (`jawOpen`, `eyeBlinkLeft`, `eyeBlinkRight`) and prepares normalized position/normal deltas without modifying the original GLB. `sampleAvatar.ts` records triangle indices and barycentric weights once and interpolates those deltas onto sampled particles and every connection endpoint, including source contours. The shared renderer applies them in its vertex shaders through a small weight array. There is no per-frame resampling, geometry upload or React state update for facial animation. The static study keeps the original sampler path; the talking preview and portfolio introduction both enable the morph shader on their own rendering copies.

`talkingMotion.ts` measures the recording's RMS audio energy in 20 ms windows, removes a noise floor, and smooths jaw opening with a short attack and longer release. Jaw motion is an amplitude-based speech approximation, not phoneme/viseme synthesis. Blink timing is repeatable with variable intervals, fast closing and slower reopening. Head rotations remain below two degrees and use the same media clock, so pause freezes the entire pose and seeking evaluates the right moment directly. Reduced motion disables blinking, head motion and electrical pulses; explicitly played audio still drives the jaw. Hiding the tab pauses playback. WebGL failure leaves audio and captions usable.

**Verified source limitation:** the existing targets are placeholder translations, not anatomically correct expressions. `jawOpen` moves both lip rims and part of the nose by the same amount, so it does not reliably open the mouth. `eyeBlinkLeft` does not move several actual upper eyelid vertices; `eyeBlinkRight` shifts upper and lower eyelid vertices together, so the eyes do not fully close. The original-shape comparison mode faithfully uses those exported deltas. The preview defaults to localized in-memory corrections to those same three shapes: preserve the nose and upper lip while the lower lip/chin move, and bring paired canonical MediaPipe eyelid boundaries together with a local skin falloff. The neutral face and original GLB remain unchanged. The **Use original GLB morph shapes for comparison** checkbox restores the literal exported targets. Corrections are gated to the named 478-vertex MediaPipe POC; other models retain their own authored shapes. This phase creates no full rig or facial reconstruction.

`morphChannels.ts` shares named channel definitions across loading, sampling and shader generation. `RecordedMotionFrame` / `recordedPose` form an adapter for future local MediaPipe animation tracks with named blendshapes and head rotation. The current GPU attribute implementation handles the three selected shapes; many more shapes would need a packed texture or combined-delta strategy while reusing the same surface bindings.

Validation: `node scripts/check-talking-avatar.mjs` verifies nonempty morphs, preservation of the neutral face, lower-lip opening with a stationary nose/upper lip, complete eyelid closure at full blink, and exact particle/pathway attachment to simultaneously deformed source triangles on desktop and compact settings, silence handling, pause/seek determinism, reduced motion, caption timing and the recorded-motion adapter. Also run `node scripts/check-avatar.mjs`, `node scripts/check-identity.mjs` and `npm run build`. The model, Neural Core, portfolio introduction and original `?avatar=prototype` viewer remain separate from this playback experiment.

## Meet Hector voice playback

The home introduction plays `public/audio/hector-introduction.mp3` (Hector’s supplied ~24-second MP3), loaded through Vite’s `BASE_URL`. This is an intentionally public playback asset; the original private reference video and FLAME workspace remain ignored. The original GLB is unchanged. `NeuralIdentity` loads a normalized copy with the same localized jaw/lid corrections as the talking preview; a future model without compatible morph targets still renders statically.

`utils/introductionVoice.ts` unlocks Web Audio synchronously within Meet Hector or Replay’s click, loads/decodes the MP3 once, and reuses `analyzeSpeech` and `speechPose`. Speech starts after the existing face assembly (4.6 seconds, immediately with reduced motion); decoding time is included in that delay. A stalled face download allows voice/text fallback after eight seconds once audio has loaded. The audio context clock drives jaw motion, blinking and subtle head movement; all particle and Plexus endpoints retain their barycentric attachment to the deformed surface. Cursor following remains intact. This is amplitude-driven jaw animation, not phoneme/viseme or facial tracking.

Pause/Play voice preserves playback position; Replay resets both audio and Core assembly. Close, Escape and section navigation stop playback immediately and cancel pending loads/schedules. Muting also silences the introduction through a dedicated gain node. Hiding the page pauses the voice until Play is clicked again. The face and text remain available when the MP3 ends; the jaw returns to neutral. The introduction’s existing copy is preserved, and the old 28-second recording’s captions are not applied to this different MP3.

With reduced motion, only the explicitly played jaw deformation continues; blinking, speech head movement and environmental effects remain disabled. The canvas renders frames while the voice is active so reduced-motion speech still animates, then returns to demand rendering. No per-frame React state updates, new dependencies, backend, synthetic voice generation or external upload is involved. Playback failure leaves a Retry voice control and usable introduction/navigation.

Checks: `node scripts/check-introduction-voice.mjs`, all three avatar checks above, and `npm run build`. The voice lifecycle check covers synchronous gesture unlock, assembly delay, pause/resume of both delayed and active speech, playback-clock morphs, mute, reduced motion, cancellation during download, replay without overlap, natural completion and retry.

## Portfolio face appearance control

Face controls are available inside Meet Hector. Inside the introduction, the eye button with its current style label cycles **Neural → Particles → Solid**. The selected style stays in memory across closing/reopening the introduction. Neural keeps the current sparse connections and faint depth shell; Particles uses a dedicated balanced facial sampling presentation without pathways; Solid uses an opaque neutral clay shader on the same facial surface. The Solid mode does not add skin textures, skull geometry or a new model.

The style is independent of the Core's appearance and of the voice controller. Changing styles neither restarts the recording nor replays the Core transition. All three presentations share the existing jaw/blink morphs and cursor following. The isolated prototype and talking study retain their original settings. Solid shading reuses the same morph vertex shader; particle bindings are resampled only when switching between the existing sampling presentations, not every frame.

## Idle facial presence

The portfolio face continues blinking and making very small attentive nods/tilts while its voice is idle, paused, loading, waiting, ended or unavailable. `idlePose` provides an unbounded deterministic blink schedule with varying intervals and occasional double blinks. It never opens the jaw or reads microphone/video input. During speech, the original audio-driven jaw remains in control. The home renderer eases blink and head transitions, and gently closes the jaw when speech stops; cursor following still works.

This uses only the existing blink morph targets and rigid head movement, shared by all three face styles. A smile requires a suitable additional morph target and is not synthesized by distorting the jaw. Closing the introduction disables idle presence. Hidden tabs stop rendering; reduced motion disables idle blinking and head movement while explicitly played audio can still drive the jaw. The isolated talking preview keeps its existing media-clock pause behavior.

The talking-avatar check now additionally validates ongoing idle blinking over two minutes, a neutral silent jaw, bounded head movements, long-session behavior and complete reduced-motion suppression.

## Refined particle portrait

The portfolio's Particles style opts into `portraitParticles`. It uses 6,200 points on desktop and 3,200 on compact devices, including a small fixed set of contour dots. Surface density is uniform rather than using the bust sampler's upper-body/front weighting. Stratified triangle-area quantiles reduce random density fluctuations between triangles. Finer, more consistent point sizes, restrained cyan/blue colors, balanced lighting and no individual sparkles keep the facial plane quieter while retaining brightness.

`particleContours.ts` samples actual boundary edges around the existing MediaPipe mask's lip and eyelid openings. It creates no new geometry and does not fill the openings. These points retain the same source-edge barycentric bindings as other animated samples, so blinking and speech keep their contours attached. Contour selection is specific to the named 478-vertex facial topology; other models fall back to balanced surface sampling. The particle-only presentation skips building a hidden Plexus graph.

The profile is opt-in: Neural and Solid portfolio modes, the Core, and the isolated studies keep their existing sampling/material defaults. The particle morph checks cover desktop and compact settings, deterministic sampling, lower-face coverage, contour presence and exact attachment to the deformed source.

### Optional face contours

Neural and Particles modes expose a **Contours on/off** button directly after the style control in the top toolbar. It hides the tagged contour dots in Particles, and the separately tagged source-boundary outline segments in Neural, through a shader uniform. Regular neural connections remain visible. Solid does not show a contour option. Main surface samples, their morph attributes, the jaw/blink animation and the audio clock are unchanged. Toggling does not rebuild or resample geometry. The preference remains in memory when changing styles or closing/reopening the introduction. The underlying face geometry and all speech/blink channels are unaffected.

### Responsive face settings

Face appearance options live in the introduction toolbar, next to mute and close. Desktop shows the style cycle followed immediately by the face-contour toggle inline for Neural and Particles; Solid only shows its style control. At mobile/tablet widths below 768px, a settings icon opens a compact vertical panel with a native appearance selector and the contour toggle when Neural or Particles is selected. Playback and navigation actions remain below the introduction copy.

The panel closes with its close button, the settings trigger, an outside click or Escape. Escape dismisses the panel before the introduction and returns focus to the trigger. Opening focuses the appearance selector; the existing introduction focus trap includes all panel controls. Switching to desktop closes a mobile panel, and portfolio shortcuts are ignored while operating the native selector. Appearance settings do not reset audio, animation or the chosen contour preference.
